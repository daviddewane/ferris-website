01 · Who this is for

The ideal workplace for founders

We are building the ideal workplace for founders. By founder, we mean a missionary: someone who feels compelled to work on a particular challenge, and starts a company as a way of tackling it.

The model is a members' club. Founders join and get access to an optimized workplace and its full set of amenities, physical and digital.

More on our founders

Founders are fanatics. They have set out to build something that matters, and the work of building it is relentless.

What they do

At the core, a CEO's job comes down to three things, and founders context-switch between them several times a day, every day. They are, in effect, always tracking their own progress across all three at once: you are what you do, and if you're not moving one of these forward, something's off.

  • Recruiting. Attaining talent, and increasingly, automating the work that doesn't need a human. This is the people side (hiring, managing, resolving conflict with cofounders and teammates, with all the personal exposure and vulnerability that comes with it) and, increasingly, the machine side: founders collaborating with AI spend real time maintaining their agents too.
  • Vision. The core creative work: refining the product, refining sales, discovery. Developing the product, structuring a deal, solving problems that don't have templates.
  • Money. Keeping enough in the bank. Talking to clients (mostly B2B; clients make you work for the check, through negotiation, service, and ongoing relationship maintenance), talking to investors, updating pitch decks, running a go-to-market motion. Rent has to be made; teammates have to be supported.

Day to day this shows up as emails, brainstorming, calls, meetings, and deep work. A lot of context-switching, a lot of uncertainty, and every day a little different. Earlier-stage founders are more reactive than proactive, simply responding to what the day throws at them; as the company matures, founders get more room to be proactive across all three verticals.

All of this work runs on decisions. Executives are paid to make a few high-quality decisions in a career. Founders make several a day, plus a thousand micro-decisions in between, amid constant context switching that burns energy and constant distraction from email, messages, and interruptions.

What they need

Focus. Minimize distractions. Eliminate unimportant decisions before they reach the founder.

Executive capacity. The endurance to make endless decisions depends on the body and brain having what they need: energy (sleep, nutrition, exercise), regulation (stress management, emotional health, recovery), and reasoning (knowledge, reflection, exposure to diverse perspectives).

A sustainable edge. Founders are in a continuous competition, played over years or decades. What gives them a competitive advantage: better tools, better focus, better prioritization.

From needs to spaces

Each need has a physical expression in the building:

  • Focus: deep focus rooms with full sensory blockout; acoustic zoning that separates heads-down areas from conversation; the call room and bookable conference rooms, so meetings never bleed into work areas.
  • Energy: the gym, the commercial kitchen and communal table, the nap room. Food and rest available without leaving the building or breaking flow.
  • Regulation: the spa (sauna, cold plunge), the open deck, the vestibule as a decompression zone between street and house.
  • Reasoning: the Vault and its member-annotated library, formal meeting rooms for mentor sessions, the long table and lounge where diverse perspectives collide by design.

The design implication

The founder is always short on time. Every element of the building, physical and digital, should streamline their day: remove friction, protect focus, and support the energy, regulation, and reasoning that peak performance depends on, day after day.

02 · The building + the AI layer

Two ideas, one system

1. The physical building is designed to maximize focus and wellness.

2. It is layered with an in-house AI system, integrated into the building itself, designed to maximize scalability and productivity.

Neither works alone. The building gives founders energy; the AI gives them time. Together they compound.

More on the AI system

Capabilities

  • Every member is paired with their own personal AI: Ferris, same name as the building, because they're one thing.
  • It learns how each member works, and picks up their voice, their priorities, and their rhythms.
  • It handles the repetitive work: the follow-ups, the research, the calendar Tetris, the drafts a founder would otherwise labor over. It drafts the investor follow-up in the member's voice while they're heads-down on product, pulls the last three exchanges with a candidate the night before an interview, and notices patterns, building a Sunday-evening briefing without being asked.
  • Over time, it anticipates: surfacing what a member needs before they ask.

Interface

The system works as an ongoing conversation: it conveys information to people and collects information from them, like speaking and listening. It lives where members already work (inbox, calendar, Slack, docs), and appears in the building through voice, wearable tech, augmented reality, and spatial interfaces. The entry sequence is a major interaction point: a personalized mirror interface showing each member tailored information and guidance. AI is woven most deeply into the work floors and classroom on three and four; the fifth floor incorporates it to a lesser degree.

Design implications

The system relies on continuous data inputs, especially visual and audio, which means the design must deliberately create privacy where it matters: ultra-private “Faraday box” rooms where members can step away from data collection entirely; a “dark mode” that disables collection in designated areas or conditions; and clear visual indicators whenever collection is occurring.

The physical design carries the infrastructure: camera placement and angles, wireless network design, sensor integration, and the design of the entry sequence. The spaces themselves should blend tech-forward, nature-forward, and human-forward environments, and craft experiences that deepen people's relationship with the system.

Trust is architecture, not policy: approval-required defaults, per-action controls, a full audit log of every action taken on a member's behalf. Members control what their Ferris sees, what it does, and what it never touches.

03 · The goal

A eudaemonia machine

The goal, stated plainly: create a eudaemonia machine: a place where a single founder theoretically has everything they need to build a billion-dollar company.

The building contains a deliberate mix of these space types, so that every need a founder encounters during their day is accommodated:

aGallerySpaces of inspiration you move through: the entry gallery's museum element, the Core's exhibit of Chicago invention.
bSalonSpaces of conversation and energy: the fifth-floor lounge and bar, communal dining, the speakeasy below.
cLibrarySpaces of accumulated knowledge: the Vault's curated collection, the classroom open to the city.
dOfficeSpaces of production: the offices and open desks of floors three and four, ready from day one.
eDeep Work ChamberSpaces of total concentration: deep focus rooms, call rooms, places to retreat and go all in.
04 · A day in the life

One founder, one day

Scroll through a typical day. Every space and amenity named here exists in the program.

10:00 AM
10:00 AM · The threshold

You cross the vestibule, a decompression zone between the street and the house, into the gallery, where the Ferris mirror interface greets you with your day, tailored. It started the night before: a quick check-in on what you got done, what's next, and a wildcard moment of reflection, so that by morning, today is already waiting for you. It has the quality of freshly squeezed orange juice and the morning paper.

10:10 AM · The coat room

A stop at the coat room: shoes, coat, bag, whatever you carried in from the street stays here. After the commute, it's the moment you shed the outside world and step into the house. You walk into the office light, and the house stays uncluttered because everyone does the same.

10:15 AM · The brief

The team gathers in a conference room for the morning brief: set goals, review progress, establish what today is for. Your Ferris listens, and minutes after you break, everyone has the summary: decisions made, actions assigned.

10:30 AM · Client call

Your Ferris nudges you: the client call is in five. The call room is quiet and equipped. Plug in and go.

11:30 AM · Your desk

Back at your desk, your Ferris has flagged the few emails that actually need you. You clear them, then you and your cofounder pull chairs together and build.

12:30 PM · Flow

Hunger hits mid-flow, and you're not about to stop. You tell your Ferris you're hungry; it sends your go-to order to the commercial kitchen on five. Thirty minutes later, lunch is at your desk and you never broke stride. Between bites, you ask your Ferris to book a call room for 2:00 PM.

2:00 PM · Client call

Another client call in a call room: camera, screen, acoustics all seamless. No fumbling.

2:30 PM · The reset

Twenty minutes in the nap room.

3:00 PM · The Inner Circle

A mentor session in a formal meeting room: a space with a sense of importance, reserved for meaningful moments.

4:00 PM · The debrief

Back at your desk for the team's end-of-day brief: progress shared, insights surfaced. Your Ferris listens and sends everyone a summary of what moved, what's blocked, and what tomorrow starts with, so the morning brief picks up exactly where today left off.

4:15 PM · Deep work

Meetings have calmed down. In your Ferris, you glance at a summary of your short- and long-term goals to stay oriented, then take the day's open thread to a deep focus room, with complete sensory blockout, and grind. Your Ferris holds your messages until you surface.

6:00 PM · The workout

Down to the gym: a quick session on the motor-less treadmill, strength work on the all-in-one machine. Curated, efficient, focused. Your Ferris shows your suggested workout for the day, built from your goals.

6:30 PM · Recovery

Sauna, cold plunge, then a Vitamin C shower in the spa. The transition from wellness back to work is part of the design.

7:00 PM · The long table

Dinner from the restaurant at the communal table on five: a gathering at someone's home, not a restaurant. Then your laptop, and a coffee from the lounge, out to the open deck, with the city in view.

8:00 PM · The night shift

Back at your desk, your Ferris has sent a prioritization report: what's open, what's urgent, and what can wait until morning. The evening is yours. Usually it goes to your highest-ROI client work; sometimes it's for clearing the administrative backlog. Nobody is pushing. This is when the work happens.

11:30 PM · End of day

You head down through the gallery, stopping once more at the mirror interface, which shows you what the day produced: the calls held, the threads closed, the ground gained. Tomorrow, it starts again.

05 · Program

A walk through the building

From the ground down, then up. The Core, a vertical channel of light and living things, threads the top three floors together: moving through it should feel like moving through an exhibit, not using a stairwell.

Ground · The threshold

Arrival as ritual

Entering should feel like crossing into a different mode of thinking and working, the way you enter an Asian household: intentional, respectful, welcoming. A private club, not a workplace lobby.

Room by room
Vestibule

A decompression zone: environmental transition, shoe cleaning, a shift in light.

Gallery

Doorman station, personalized mirror interface, a curated museum installation that changes the daily moment of arrival, screens for events and new-member highlights, a package nook, a DoorDash window.

Below ground · The wellness floor

Body and mind

A private athletic club, not a commercial gym: calm, elevated, restorative. Beside it, a gym for the mind. Behind a concealed door, a hidden members club.

Room by room
Gym

A curated selection of highly effective tools. Premium, efficient, focused.

Locker rooms & spa

Changing, lockers, grooming, a premium shower experience. Closer to a high-end spa than a workplace amenity.

Sauna & cold plunge

Heat and cold as daily rituals: recovery, meditation, resilience, and the relationship-building that happens there.

The Vault: brain gym

A curated library of books, papers, and member recommendations with personal notes; puzzles and cognitive games for warm-ups; creative stimulation; and material chosen to push members past their current expertise. A private intellectual club: inspiring, contemplative, curiosity-driven.

The Side Vault: speakeasy

Concealed entry, poker, backgammon, a bar, a DJ setup. An old-world club with the edge of a modern technology lair.

Laundry

Commercial washer and dryer, utility sink, linen and supply storage, sited near the locker rooms. A second point on the fifth floor is under consideration.

Floors three & four · The work floors

The quiet engine

These are the floors where the work happens.

Room by room
Private offices & open desks

Offices for teams of three or more, designated open desks for smaller teams, each with a standardized setup: sit/stand desk, ergonomic chair, monitor, keyboard, mouse, HDMI adapter.

Conference rooms & casual meeting spaces

Bookable conference rooms where calls feel seamless; collaboration spaces with writable surfaces. The casual meeting space typology may be eliminated once the fifth-floor lounge is established, to preserve a quieter environment on three and four.

Call rooms

Strong acoustic isolation, occupancy indicators: conversations that stay confidential.

Deep focus & safe rooms

Acoustic isolation, visual privacy, minimal distraction. Quiet, protected rooms where members retreat for uninterrupted concentration or a mental reset within an otherwise open floor.

Classroom / pre-seed space

The use of this space is currently flexible. Some ideas: an educational space for our members, running courses, workshops, and lectures; an occasional venue for fitness classes when the event space is occupied; or a home for promising pre-seed founders who cannot yet afford the membership fee, on a trial period.

Residents' lab & maker space

A workshop for Residents, with tools for prototyping and creating. An experimental spirit and an energetic atmosphere.

Nap room

A quiet space for rest, meditation, and decompression, with naps in Japanese capsule pods. Acoustic privacy, calming light: peaceful and restorative.

Kids room

Supervised play and quiet activities for founders with families. Safe materials, child-proofing, visibility, age-appropriate zones, acoustic separation.

Pantries, coat rooms & restrooms

One of each per floor. Member pantries that feel like a high-quality home kitchen rather than an office break room: coffee, beverages, snacks, personal food storage. Coat rooms for coats, lockers and cubbies, umbrellas, bags, and bikes. And restrooms that are individually private and reliably stocked, closer to a well-designed hotel restroom or makeup counter than an office facility. Small details where they matter most.

The fifth floor · The living room

Where the house gathers

Food, conversation, celebration, and the rooms reserved for meaningful moments. Warm and communal by day; by night, the energy of the whole house rises here.

Room by room
Commercial kitchen

It powers an in-house restaurant serving high-quality meals all day, with efficient service and flexible ordering.

Communal dining

Long-table dinners and informal meetings that feel like a gathering at someone's home.

Event space

A highly flexible room: movable furniture, stage and AV, lighting controls, shifting from founder talks and AI events to celebrations and fitness classes without losing atmosphere.

Lounge & bar

A private club lounge built for meaningful conversation. One idea worth exploring: making it convertible day to night, coffee giving way to a full-service bar.

Open deck

Outdoor work, breaks, and reflection, with the city in view.

Radio/DJ booth & media studio

Acoustically isolated, able to open to the event space: podcasts, music for the floor, content creation.

Formal meeting rooms

Acoustic privacy and high-quality finishes, reserved for investor meetings, mentor conversations, partner meetings, and sensitive discussions. A sense of importance and deference: a special room for meaningful moments, not a generic conference room.

Coat room

Coat storage, lockers and cubbies, umbrella and bag storage, keeping the event space free of clutter.

Circulation

The connective tissue

The journeys between floors are designed with the same intention as the floors themselves.

The Core & the elevators
The Core

A central column that physically and emotionally connects the building: biophilic, atrium-like, with an exhibit on the third-floor landing highlighting Chicago's inventors, entrepreneurs, history, and culture. A major moment that matters: inspiring and energizing, creating a sense of pride and connection to Chicago's legacy of innovation.

Elevators

Efficient, comfortable, reliable vertical circulation. Seamless.

07 · Aesthetic direction

How it should feel

Comfortable

“I want this to be a place where people are comfortable.”

Welcoming, supportive

Safety is a prerequisite for productivity: when the foundational need for security is met, people are free to explore, grow, and create. We cultivate that safety through hospitality considered to an extreme degree: the warm greeting on arrival, the fully stocked makeup counter, the matcha machine for the community's matcha fanatics, the complementary gift. Small gestures that, in aggregate, communicate intentionality and generosity. Moments that matter.

Biophilic

Connection to nature nurtures wellness. Sunlight, plants and living elements, natural materials: a space that feels alive and grounded.

08 · Existing strengths

What we're building on

Chicago

The third largest city in America, with no shortage of talent, but no physical nucleus for entrepreneurship.

What it makes possible: rather than competing with Silicon Valley on capital, we compete by creating the best place in the world to build a company. That means providing the conditions founders need to build freely: freedom to take risks, psychological safety, inspiration, productivity. Talent attracts talent, great companies emerge, investors follow. Our first customer is the authentic entrepreneur, not the investor.

The building

We have complete control over its design, including the Core, a vertical circulation channel with the potential to unify every floor.

What it makes possible: the freedom, and the responsibility, to be ambitious, experiment thoughtfully, and shape every single aspect of the experience with intention. Even the everyday journey between floors can become a bespoke, place-specific moment that matters.

The team

Excellence in workplace architecture (David), hospitality design (Barker/Nestor), operations and community (Rox), technology (David S.), and business and strategy (Anuraag), underpinned by a shared spirit of generosity.

What it makes possible: every layer of the experience, physical, operational, and digital, designed by people at the top of their craft, working as one team.

The AI layer

A first-of-its-kind home for builders, elevated by an AI integration that is first-of-its-kind in the world.

What it makes possible: personalization of experiences, workflows, and operational systems at a scale that would be impossible through human effort alone. Rather than replacing people, AI amplifies founders' ability to focus on meaningful work by tailoring the environment to how they work best.

An optimized work environment, designed by the best of the best.

A personalized in-house AI system, woven into the building itself.

This pairing has never existed before.

The result is a kind of chemistry: a space where great companies emerge.
